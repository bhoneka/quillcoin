THE GUIDE · EVERYTHING, EXPLAINED

What is QuillCoin?+

A treasure hunt on 2b2t, the oldest anarchy server in Minecraft. Signed books are hidden in dungeon chests across the overworld. Every book carries a code that works exactly once. Whoever types that code here first owns one QuillCoin, written QLL.

There is nothing to buy. No presale, no shop, no starter pack. The only way a coin comes into existence is somebody finding a book.

It is a game. Nobody here promises that a coin is, or ever will be, worth anything. If people decide to trade them, that is theirs to decide.

Where are the books?+

In ordinary dungeons in the overworld: the small mossy cobblestone rooms with a monster spawner in the middle and a chest or two against the wall. They sit somewhere in the ring between 15,000 and 100,000 blocks from spawn, in any direction. Every round publishes its own ring before it opens, on its yellow bar, and the ring cannot change afterwards.

That is everything anybody knows. The person who hid them flew there with a black screen and never saw a coordinate; this site has never seen one either. There are no hints and there will never be any.

A book is an item like any other. It can be taken by whoever opens the chest first, and it can be destroyed along with the chest.

How does a book get hidden?+

One person hides every book, by hand, with a tool that keeps them blind the whole way. Every hide goes the same:

  1. One key, in the nether. The recording starts by itself, and for the first seconds you still see the nether the hider is standing in. Then the tool draws a random point inside the round's ring, shows it to nobody, the hider included, and stops drawing the world.
  2. A blind flight. The tool flies there on its own. The screen is black the whole way; all it says is roughly how many minutes are left.
  3. Through a portal. The hider lands, builds a portal and steps into the overworld. The land around them is not drawn. The only thing the tool draws is the first dungeon with a chest that it finds near the point.
  4. The book. The hider digs to that dungeon and opens its chest. One more key: the tool makes the code, writes the book, has the server sign it and puts it in the chest. The code is never on the screen, and the hider cannot open the book.
  5. Pulled away by a pearl. The hider does not walk or fly home. An ender pearl that was thrown long before and is kept waiting far away is set off, and the hider is pulled to it in an instant. They leave without ever having seen the way there or the way back.
  6. Only then does the book's fingerprint appear on this page. The recording runs a few seconds longer, so you see the hider arrive somewhere else, and is then put on the book's card.

The tool outlines every block that was broken on the way in, so that it can be put back before leaving.

The first two books of round 1 were hidden before the tool did all of this by itself: the first recording begins once the world is hidden and stops a moment before the pearl, the second was cut by hand a moment after it.

Is the book in my hand real?+
  • Hover it in your inventory. The author line must read by QuillCoin. Nobody else can sign a book under that name, but a lookalike name is easy to make (a capital I in place of an l), so do not stop here.
  • Its title names its round and number, like R1 Coin 37, and the same round and number are on this page.
  • Type the code from page three into the box at the top. The site answers while you type, before you press anything: real and unspent, already redeemed (with the name of who did it, and when), or no coin has this code.

The code is the real test: a fake book cannot carry a code this site knows. Checking never spends a code. Only the REDEEM button does.

How do I redeem it?+
  1. Type the code. It is on page three of the book. The dashes place themselves; capitals or not makes no difference.
  2. Sign in with Discord. The coin will belong to that Discord account.
  3. Enter your Minecraft name and press Enter. It is the name shown on the coin's card.
  4. Press REDEEM. If something is still missing, it lights up red.

Then the book's card turns gold, the chest it sat in appears on the map, 1 QLL lands on your account, and 0.1 QLL goes to the founder. It costs you nothing, and you need no wallet for any of it.

If somebody typed the same code before you, the site tells you who and when. First redemption wins; there is no second place.

Why Discord? Why my Minecraft name?+

Discord is the owner. A coin has to belong to someone, and a Discord account is something nearly every player already has. Sign in again with the same account, on any device, and your coins are there.

From Discord the site receives your display name, your avatar and the email address on the account. The email is only there because sign-in needs it: it is never shown and never used for anything else. The site cannot read your messages, your servers or your friends.

The Minecraft name is a label. It goes on the coin's card and on the map. The site does not check that the name is yours, so it proves nothing by itself. What proves a find is the code.

Moving coins to a wallet+

Not open yet. Moving coins to a wallet starts once the token exists on Solana. Everything below is how it will work.

A wallet is an app that holds tokens under keys only you have. Phantom, Solflare and Backpack are free and all work. The app gives you an address: a long string of letters and digits, safe to share, like an account number.

  1. Copy your address from the wallet app.
  2. Paste it under MOVE YOUR COINS TO A WALLET, which appears in the redeem section once you own a coin.
  3. Press the button, read the address back, press again.

Your whole site balance is sent in one go, and the site pays the network fee. Once the coins are in your wallet nobody else can touch them: not this site, not the founder.

A transfer cannot be undone. Use a wallet you hold the keys to, never the deposit address of an exchange, and check the first and last four characters before you confirm.

Your wallet shows nothing? Some wallets hide tokens they have not seen before. Add QLL by its token address, which is printed under the claim button. While everything runs on the test network, also switch the wallet to its test mode (often called devnet or developer mode).

The site remembers your wallet. The address you claimed to last is kept with your Discord account and filled in the next time. Only you see it there. The transfer itself is public, like everything on Solana: anyone can see which address received coins, and when.

If a transfer hangs, your coins are not lost and not doubled. They stay reserved until the network says how it ended: arrived, or back on the site.

The words that open your wallet (its recovery phrase) are yours alone. Nobody from QuillCoin will ever ask for them. Anybody who does is robbing you.

The founder's tenth+

Every find creates 1 QLL for the finder and 0.1 QLL for the founder. That tenth is the founder's only income. It was published before the first coin existed, the founder's wallet is public, and both amounts are created in the same transaction, so anyone can watch them arrive together.

The founder sells nothing and holds no coins from before the hunt. Coins exist only because books were found.

You do not have to take that on trust. The ledger page counts the coins that exist on Solana and compares them with the finds written in the ledger. If a single coin were ever created without a find, the two numbers would stop matching, in public.

Can the hider cheat?+
  • The hider never sees a code. The hiding tool writes and signs the book itself; the code exists in memory for a moment and is never shown, printed or logged. Coin books cannot be opened while the tool is on.
  • The hider never sees a coordinate. The tool picks a random point and flies there with the screen painted black. On the ground the terrain is not drawn at all; the only thing shown is the dungeon the run ended at.
  • Nothing can be added later. The fingerprint of every book is published on this page before its round opens. Once a round is open, the site refuses new books for it.
  • The hider's accounts can never redeem. They are on a blacklist inside the database.
  • Every hide is recorded, from the moment the run starts until a few seconds after the hider has been pulled away from the chest, so you see them leave. The hiding tool starts and stops the recording itself, so there is no choosing what gets filmed. The recording plays on the coin's card, next to its fingerprint: download the file, hash it, compare. Once the round is open it can never be swapped for another cut.

The tool is open source, so all of this can be read line by line.

The honest limit: nothing that runs on somebody's own computer can be made impossible to tamper with. What the tool does is make tampering visible. Every run, every voided run and every stash is written to an audit log that is published with its round, next to the recordings and the fingerprints.

What is a fingerprint (a hash)?+

A way of turning any text into a short line of letters and digits. The same text always gives the same fingerprint, the smallest change gives a completely different one, and nobody can work backwards from a fingerprint to the text. The kind used here is called SHA-256.

This site stores the fingerprint of every code and never the code itself. When you type a code, its fingerprint is worked out and looked up. That is why the full list of books can be public without giving a single code away, and why nobody, the site included, can redeem a book they have not held.

Why publish them before the hunt? Because it fixes the list. A book that turns up later has to match a fingerprint that was already here, so nobody, the hider included, can slip in an extra book or swap a code once the hunt is on. It is the sealed envelope with the answer in it, handed over before the quiz starts.

A code is 20 characters long, which makes more possible codes than there are grains of sand on a hundred billion Earths. Guessing one is not a plan.

Codes never contain the letters I, L, O or U. A 1 is always a one and a 0 is always a zero.

The map, the ghosts and the glyphs+

The gold ring on the map is where a round's books are: somewhere between the dashed circle and the solid one. That is as close as anyone can point.

Gold books on the map are found books, each at the chest it was found in. When a book is hidden, the tool locks the chest's position in a box that only the book's own code can open. The site keeps the box and cannot look inside. Redeeming the code opens it, and the book appears on the map.

Grey books are the ones still out there. Their number is real; their positions are made up. They jump to a new random spot every few seconds, because nobody knows where they are.

Glyphs on a coin's card stand for what nobody knows yet: where it is, who will find it, when. They never settle until the book is redeemed.

Coordinates are overworld coordinates. The nether number next to them is the same place divided by eight.

The ledger+

Every coin that was ever created, and every move to a wallet, is one line in a public ledger. Lines can only be added. Each line carries the fingerprint of the line before it, so changing or removing one would break every line after it.

Whenever coins are sent to a wallet, the newest fingerprint is written into that Solana transaction, where nobody can edit it, the founder included. The ledger page checks the whole chain inside your own browser, and reads those transactions straight from Solana.

Trading, stealing and losing books+

The code is the coin. Whoever types it first gets it, however they came by the book. Found, looted, stolen, traded: the site does not ask.

Books can be copied. Minecraft lets anyone copy a written book, and a copy carries the same code. If you trade for a book that has not been redeemed, the other side may have kept the code. Check it on this page first, and redeem the moment it is in your hands.

Never show page three. Not in a screenshot, not on a stream, not in a video. Anyone who can read the code can redeem it.

Burned coins stay burned. A book destroyed with its chest never becomes a coin. Some of the grey books on this page may already be gone. That is the hunt.

Your Discord account is the key to coins that are still on the site. Moving them to a wallet makes them independent of it.

Rounds and coin numbers+

Books are hidden in rounds. While a round is being hidden, its fingerprints pile up on this page. Then the round opens, its list is closed for good, and its books can be redeemed.

Coin numbers are drawn at random between 1 and 1000, so a number says nothing about where a book is or when it was hidden. Coin 37 is not older, closer or easier than coin 912.

Every round has its own yellow bar above. Press it and its books drop out.
